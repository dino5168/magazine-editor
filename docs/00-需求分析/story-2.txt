After that night, Nora climbed the stairs with him every evening. He taught her how to trim a wick, how to read the barometer, how to tell a fishing boat from a cargo ship by the pattern of its lights. He taught her the names of the stars that sailors steered by, and the names of the ships that had sunk on the reef before the lighthouse was built. She wrote everything down in a notebook with a green cover, and slowly the notebook filled.

She also began to notice things on her own. The way the gulls went quiet an hour before a storm. The way the fog crept in from the east like a cat that did not want to be seen. The way her grandfather rubbed his left hand when the weather turned cold, and pretended he wasn't.

In October, the storm came.

It arrived faster than the radio had promised, a black wall moving across the water. By late afternoon the waves were breaking over the lower rocks, and the whole tower hummed with the wind. Elias started up the stairs at the usual time, but halfway up he stopped, gripped the rail, and sat down heavily on a step.
